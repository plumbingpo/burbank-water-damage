Crawl Space Water Removal: What Burbank Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Rot You Cannot See

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

The pump out itself is normally done in a few hours. Expect 3 to 5 days after that for the lumber, with dehumidification and air movement going the whole time. Thick beams, a dirt floor with no liner, or water that sat for weeks can add time. We go by moisture readings, not by the calendar.
Mold can begin on damp wood and paper faced insulation within 24 to 48 hours. Little air moves down there and no sun reaches it, so natural drying is very slow. Removing the water and lowering the humidity quickly is the best way to limit growth. Where growth has already started, we clean the wood and treat it with an antimicrobial.

It is best to stay out as long as there is standing water. Electrical cables and boxes are common down there, and wet ones can energize the water.
