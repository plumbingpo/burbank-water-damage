Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Burbank Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet

Structural Drying: What Homeowners Ask

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days. Building construction, ambient conditions, the materials involved and the degree of saturation all change how long it takes. When saturation is heavy or the property is commercial, expect 5 to 7 days or beyond. Our technician checks progress every day and updates you on timing.
Psychrometry is the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Our IICRC-certified technicians use it to calculate equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns for each job. No two materials release moisture at the same rate, so the approach varies. Getting it right avoids both under-drying, which feeds mold, and over-drying, which can damage wood.
Relying on a few fans is the leading reason secondary mold shows up after water damage. Commercial LGR dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential units. Evaporation from the structure is sped up by high-velocity air movers set for precise airflow. And unless you measure with a meter, you cannot tell when the job is done.
Your electricity use will go up while the commercial drying equipment is running. In most cases that works out to an additional $50 to $150 across the full job. Insurers often reimburse it as a restoration cost on your claim, though that depends on the policy.
Our technicians take readings from all the affected materials using calibrated moisture meters. We compare the numbers against reference materials from areas that stayed dry and against the ranges IICRC S500 accepts, and only then call it complete. Touch and appearance by themselves are not proof of anything.

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Hygrometers, moisture meters and a thermal camera let us map moisture across each area that was affected. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
