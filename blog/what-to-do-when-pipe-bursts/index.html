Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in Burbank, CA

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Burbank, CA

For most homeowners, a pipe bursting ranks among the worst plumbing emergencies there are. At 4 to 8 gallons every minute, one broken pipe can soak whole rooms, damage what you own, and compromise the structure in a matter of hours. What you do in the first few minutes after finding the break often decides whether you face a simple repair or a full restoration, and it can keep thousands of dollars of damage off the bill. This guide from Burbank Restoration gives Burbank, CA homeowners a clear action plan for this common emergency.

Step 1: Turn the Water Off Right Away

The single most important thing you can do when a pipe bursts is stop the flow of water as fast as possible. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Start by finding the main shutoff valve for the house. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Burbank houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. To stop water throughout the house, twist the handle clockwise (righty-tighty) until it stops. If you can identify the specific pipe that burst and it has its own shutoff valve nearby, close that valve as well.

Can't find the valve, or can't get it to turn? Phone the water utility right away and ask them to stop service at the meter. Knowing where that valve lives ahead of time, not during a crisis, matters for exactly this reason. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. If it's stuck, have a plumber service it before you need it in an emergency.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

There is useful work to do until help shows up: first, carry anything valuable, plus paperwork and electronics, to a dry room. Lift furniture off wet carpet by placing aluminum foil or plastic blocks under legs. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Mop up accessible standing water with towels. Prop cabinet and closet doors open so air can move through. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker box, but only when that is safe and you are not standing in water.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. If the burst pipe has caused significant flooding and you cannot safely access your electrical panel, leave the electricity on and wait for professionals to assess the situation. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Take Photos Before You Clean Up

Hold off on cleanup until you have a thorough record of the damage to give your insurer. Capture the affected belongings, damaged materials, and standing water in both photos and video. Write down when you found the break, both the date and the rough time, and photograph the leak's source if you can see it. Burbank Restoration supplies detailed reports as well, and paired with your notes they strengthen your claim and your case for fair payment.

How Burbank Homeowners Can Avoid Burst Pipes

Some bursts cannot be avoided, but Burbank homeowners lower the odds a great deal when they insulate exposed pipes in unheated spots such as attics, crawl spaces, and garages. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air to reach pipes. Keep an eye on water pressure, and if it runs above 80 PSI, have a pressure reducing valve put in. Braided stainless steel should take the place of worn supply lines, rubber washing machine hoses above all. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
